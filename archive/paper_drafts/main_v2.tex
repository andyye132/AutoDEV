\title{Do Discovered Exploration Procedures Generalize?\\An Autonomous Loop, the Harness Exploit It Found, and What Survives Replay}
\author{Andy Ye \\ University of Washington}

\begin{abstract}
Game studios test games with hand-designed RL agents; we ask what an autonomous research loop can discover instead --- and the answer turned into a single
finding about autonomous discovery itself: \textbf{what a loop discovers, and how far it generalizes, is determined by the evaluator it optimizes; the search is
interchangeable, the fitness is the steering wheel.} We show this six ways on a replay-verified benchmark of 800+ planted defects across five engine families,
with 30\% of levels, one whole defect class, and entire engines sealed from search. (1) An \emph{untrusted} evaluator steers into fraud: the loop's first
``discovery'' exploited a race in its own harness, passed every evaluator-mediated check, and was caught only by replaying hits from reset --- verification now
lives inside the fitness, making the exploit worthless by construction. (2) A \emph{trusted, well-aimed} evaluator beats training: LLM-written procedures
surpass every hand-designed, one-shot-LLM and PPO tester at equal budget (0.62 vs.\ 0.57 vs.\ 0.49 on the search split; 37--38\% vs.\ 29\% vs.\ 27\% on the sealed
class, paired CIs excluding zero), find each target 5--9$\times$ cheaper with zero training, and saturate with budget while PPO needs 4--16$\times$ to catch up.
(3) \emph{Headroom placement is discovery}: the same loop climbs from weak seeds, flatlines at the frontier under a plain fitness, advances the frontier only
when the fitness targets the tier where testers differ ($+0.011\pm0.009$ paired), and trades along the search--transfer frontier under regret shaping.
(4) \emph{Evaluator breadth is generality}: a multi-engine fitness yields a program that transfers at 100\% to two engine families it never saw, where every
single-engine program collapses. (5) No composition of finished programs --- fusion, portfolios, schedules, LLM routing --- does what a fitness change does; all
tie. (6) Steered well, the loop pays off at the object level: 28 replay-verified engine anomalies, including a player-triggerable escape in a shipped level and
four joint tears. The benchmark, procedures, searches and Part~1 were produced by the loop; the authors built the oracles, verified every number, and document
every intervention --- including the exploit.
\end{abstract}
