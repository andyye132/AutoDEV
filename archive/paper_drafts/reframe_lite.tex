% Do Discovered Exploration Strategies Generalize?\\Autonomous Program Search Finds Exploration Procedures That Reach Unseen Target Classes

% ---- abstract ----
% Exploration in reinforcement learning is driven by hand-designed intrinsic objectives (curiosity, novelty, coverage), and the
% field's own results say they are unreliable: random exploration beats RND asymptotically in large 3D games, more search can make a
% tester worse, and coverage anti-correlates with what testers exist to find. We ask whether an autonomous research loop can discover
% an exploration \emph{procedure} instead --- code over a save/restore archive of simulator states, the family that contains Go-Explore
% --- and whether the discovered procedure generalizes. On Kinetix, a suite of 2D physics games, we build a target-state benchmark
% from 337 planted defects (five classes, each with a witness state; random floors and a reachability calibration), seal 30\% of levels
% and one entire target class from the search, and let an LLM-driven program search write the procedure. \textbf{Qualifying result:}
% a single run of the discovered procedure reaches 72\% of targets on the search split vs.\ 59\% for the strongest hand-designed
% procedure and 56\% for the best of five PPO intrinsic-motivation testers at equal simulator budget, 64\% vs.\ 60\% on held-out
% levels, and 47\% vs.\ 30\% on the sealed target class (+0.15 paired per-instance gain, 95\% CI $\pm$0.05); normalized by the reachable
% set it finds 88\% / 94\% / 74\% of what any method reaches. Constant tuning of the discovered program recovers a tenth of its gain
% and fixed portfolios of designs lose to it, so the gain is structural; every search makes it in its first generations and then
% moves inside evaluation noise. The benchmark, the search, the baselines and Part~1 were produced by the autonomous loop; the authors
% built and verified the oracles and document every intervention.

\subsection*{1.1 Hypothesis}
Exploration objectives in RL are hand-designed, and where they have been tested as \emph{testers} --- agents whose job is to reach
states someone cares about --- they disappoint: random exploration beats RND asymptotically in large 3D maps~\cite{goexplore-reach},
more search made MCTS worse at reaching injected faults~\cite{ariyurek-mcts}, and coverage anti-correlates with the bugs a tester
surfaces~\cite{ccpt}. The one precedent for searching over the exploration algorithm itself, meta-learned curiosity~\cite{alet},
transferred across control tasks but had no game, no target-state benchmark and no held-out target class. We hypothesized that (H1)
an LLM-driven program search over exploration \emph{procedures} --- code over a save/restore archive --- reaches target states faster
than hand-designed procedures and learned intrinsic motivation at equal budget; (H2) the discovered procedure \emph{transfers} to
levels it never saw and to a target class it was never scored on; and (H3) the same procedure surfaces real, unplanted anomalies.
H2 is decisive: searched objectives are known to overfit their fitness~\cite{dmmultiagent}. Game testing supplies the targets
(planted defects with witness states) and the real anomalies that validate the oracles.
