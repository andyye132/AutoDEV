\begin{abstract}
Exploration in reinforcement learning is driven by hand-designed intrinsic objectives (curiosity, novelty, coverage), and the
field's own results say they are unreliable as testers: random exploration beats RND asymptotically in large 3D games, more search
can make a tester worse, and coverage anti-correlates with what testers exist to find. We ask whether an autonomous research loop can
discover an exploration \emph{procedure} instead --- code over a save/restore archive of simulator states --- and whether it generalizes.
On Kinetix, a suite of 2D physics games, we build a target-state benchmark from 337 planted defects (five classes, each with a witness
state, random floors and a reachability calibration), seal 30\% of levels and one entire target class from the search, and let an
LLM-driven program search write the procedure. \textbf{Qualifying result:} a single run of the discovered procedure reaches 72\% of
targets on the search split vs.\ 59\% for the strongest hand-designed procedure and 56\% for the best of five PPO intrinsic-motivation
testers at equal budget, 64\% vs.\ 60\% on held-out levels, and 47\% vs.\ 30\% on the sealed target class (+0.15 paired gain, 95\% CI
$\pm$0.05); normalized by the reachable set it finds 88\% / 94\% / 74\% of what any method reaches. Constant tuning recovers a tenth
of the gain and portfolios of designs lose to it, so the gain is structural; every search makes it early and then moves inside
evaluation noise. The benchmark, search, baselines and Part~1 were produced by the loop; the authors built and verified the oracles.
\end{abstract}
